# Submission Note

I built this as a staged sourcing workflow rather than a one-shot AI pass. The Manifest attendee list is scraped and parsed in code, then stored in SQLite with normalized company keys so reruns are repeatable and do not redo work that has already been paid for. A seed attendee file is included as a fallback because public conference pages can change or render differently during review.

The cost-control design is deliberate. The tool first runs deterministic rules over the full list to identify obvious non-prospects: large incumbents, investors, associations, universities, consultancies, retailers/brands, pure carriers, ports, trucking firms, and logistics service providers without software or platform signals. The dashboard still ranks and displays the full attendee universe, and it preserves a broad candidate count for transparency. Paid Tavily/OpenAI calls, however, default to a stricter high-priority queue that requires stronger startup or technology signals such as AI, robotics, SaaS, software, automation, platform, analytics, visibility, autonomous systems, optimization, warehouse automation, standalone WMS/TMS, retail infrastructure, healthcare operations, climate/sustainability, supply-chain technology, or startup-like `.ai` evidence. This keeps retrieval in code, narrows paid spend, and reserves the LLM for synthesis and judgment.

The UI separates baseline screening from verified prospect ranking. Generic or role-like entries such as `AI Startup`, `Startup`, `Stealth Company`, `TBD`, `Student`, `CEO`, `COO`, `Founder`, `N/A`, and `Unknown` are treated as noisy entries and are not high-priority paid-enrichment targets. Baseline-only rows stay visible in the full ranked pipeline, clearly marked as lacking external evidence, but the Overview top-prospects table is reserved for companies with cached Tavily evidence and OpenAI scoring.

The Streamlit workflow is intentionally guided: reviewers see three primary actions by default, `Load & classify companies`, `Generate verified prospects`, and `Verify cache reuse`. More granular pipeline controls and cost settings remain available in collapsed expanders, but they do not dominate the daily investor workflow.

The scoring is grounded in Wittington Ventures' focus areas and edge: commerce, healthcare, consumer, climate, and technology companies where Wittington could plausibly accelerate adoption through relationships with large Canadian brands such as Loblaw, Shoppers Drug Mart, PC Financial, PC Optimum, Choice Properties, Holt Renfrew, Joe Fresh, and Lifemark. Scores combine venture-backability, Wittington sector fit, Wittington strategic edge, stage signal, traction signal, and data confidence. Caps prevent non-startups from ranking highly just because they are large or relevant to logistics.

The Streamlit app is meant to be reviewable and operational: it exposes each pipeline stage, API limits, the high-priority paid-enrichment queue, cache behavior, metrics, filters, CSV export, and source evidence. It also runs without API keys in deterministic baseline mode, while Tavily and OpenAI keys enable the full external enrichment and LLM scoring workflow.
