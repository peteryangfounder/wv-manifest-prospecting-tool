# Future Capabilities

The next UX layer should make this feel like a daily investor workflow rather than a scoring table. I would add saved shortlists, CRM-style statuses, owner assignment, partner comments, duplicate merge review, and saved views by vertical such as commerce infrastructure, supply-chain automation, climate logistics, and healthcare operations. Each company should have a drilldown page with the source evidence, score history, why the rank changed, and a one-click memo generator for partner meetings. I would also add bulk actions: export to CRM, mark as not relevant, request deeper diligence, and create a partner intro hypothesis tied to Wittington's corporate relationships.

The most valuable external integrations would be richer company and market data APIs. Crunchbase or PitchBook would improve funding stage, investors, round timing, and venture-backability. People Data Labs, Clearbit-style enrichment, LinkedIn/headcount data, and company website crawling would improve team size, growth, category, and HQ signals. News APIs would catch recent launches, financing, partnerships, and customer wins. BuiltWith, Similarweb, job-posting data, SEC/SEDAR, corporate registries, import/export datasets, and app-store or marketplace signals would add traction and technology proof. For a Manifest-specific workflow, I would also integrate conference metadata if available: sponsors, speaker status, booth presence, attendee roles, and meeting availability.

The highest-leverage ranking improvement would come from Wittington's first-party data. CRM records would show prior contact, ownership, stage, and whether a company has already been passed on. Meeting notes would reveal qualitative history: founder quality, diligence concerns, customer references, and partner interest. Calendar and email metadata would add relationship strength and recency without needing to read every message. Past deal history and portfolio data would allow lookalike scoring against companies Wittington already backed, while also flagging competitive conflicts. Corporate partner feedback from Loblaw, Shoppers Drug Mart, PC Financial, PC Optimum, Choice Properties, Holt Renfrew, Joe Fresh, and Lifemark would be especially important because Wittington's edge is not just capital; it is helping the right startup validate, sell into, and scale through strategic relationships.

With that data, the ranking should evolve from "is this a good company?" to "is this company specifically worth Wittington's time right now?" A lower-profile startup with a clear Loblaw supply-chain use case, a warm relationship, and recent hiring momentum should outrank a famous incumbent or a generic logistics provider. The product should explain that ranking transparently so investors can trust the shortlist and quickly adjust it when their thesis changes.
