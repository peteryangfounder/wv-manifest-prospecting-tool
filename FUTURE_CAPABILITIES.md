# Future Capabilities

A production version should add persistent review state. Saved shortlists, owner assignment, partner comments, duplicate resolution, review status, and thesis-specific views would make the tool more useful for recurring sourcing work. Relevant views for Wittington could include commerce infrastructure, retail operations, health services, consumer fintech, loyalty, climate, and logistics software. The company detail page should also include source records, enrichment evidence, score components, confidence level, score history, rank changes, and the reason for each material score change. This would make it easier to separate weak prospects from companies with limited public evidence. Useful bulk actions would include CRM export, reviewer assignment, deeper diligence requests, partner-introduction notes, and first-pass memo generation with supporting evidence, unresolved questions, and proposed next steps.

External integrations should be selected based on whether they improve ranking quality, confidence, or diligence usefulness. Crunchbase or PitchBook could improve funding-stage detection, financing history, investor quality, and venture-backability assessment. People Data Labs, LinkedIn-style headcount data, Clearbit-style enrichment, and website crawling could improve team, location, category, growth, customer-segment, and product-depth signals. News APIs could surface recent launches, financing events, customer wins, partnerships, and strategic changes. BuiltWith, Similarweb, job postings, app-store data, marketplace reviews, corporate registries, SEC or SEDAR filings, and import-export datasets could add evidence about technology stack, distribution, hiring, regulatory footprint, and commercial activity. Manifest-specific metadata such as sponsor status, speaker participation, booth presence, exhibitor category, attendee seniority, and meeting availability would help prioritize companies with stronger conference intent.

Wittington’s first-party data would improve the ranking more than additional public data alone. CRM records would show prior contact, ownership, pass reasons, current status, and thesis association. Meeting notes would add diligence signals such as founder quality, customer references, pricing concerns, implementation risk, technical credibility, and partner interest. Calendar and email metadata could contribute relationship recency and relationship strength without requiring broad summarization of private correspondence. Past deal history and portfolio data could support lookalike scoring, thesis matching, and conflict detection.

Corporate partner feedback would improve the strategic-edge score. Feedback from Loblaw, Shoppers Drug Mart, PC Financial, PC Optimum, Choice Properties, Holt Renfrew, Joe Fresh, Lifemark, and related operating businesses could clarify whether a company maps to a real operating need, which buyer would own the problem, whether a pilot path exists, what procurement constraints apply, and whether the product is likely to be usable in practice.

The ranking should answer whether a company is worth Wittington’s attention in the current sourcing cycle. A lower-profile company with a clear operating use case, recent hiring, relevant relationship signals, and strong sector fit may rank above a better-known company with weaker strategic relevance. Cost reporting should also expand with each paid provider, including API calls, credits, subscription costs, per-company enrichment cost, and per-workflow spend. The product should preserve evidence, expose uncertainty, record ownership, and convert noisy external company lists into a repeatable venture-sourcing process.
